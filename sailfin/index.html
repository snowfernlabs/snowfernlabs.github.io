Field guide

Know what you caught

Species pages show the fish's painting (or an outline, for the few still waiting for art), how to tell it apart, and the fish it's most often confused with, side by side. Many have how-to-catch tips paraphrased from state and federal fish and wildlife agencies, with each source a tap away.

  • 529 freshwater and saltwater species, with ranges for all 64 states and provinces
  • 220 species with sourced catch tips
  • 55 states and provinces with official record fish
  • A typical weight for any length, from published studies for 383 species

Logging

Log it before the next cast

Your usual fish are first, and the rest is filled in from today: time, place, weather and what you usually keep. Tap the fish, tap Save.

  • Say it out loud and sort it out later
  • +1 for another of the same, from the app or a home-screen widget
  • Type a day out like a note, or log from your photos
  • Bring in an old log from a notes app or a spreadsheet

Trips

Every trip counts

Each day keeps its water, catches and notes. With location on, the weather, tides, water temperature and river flow fill in by themselves, even if you had no signal on the water. Skunked? Log it anyway.

Track rods, reels and line too, if you like: what each one caught and how old the line is.

Insights and goals

Look back on your season

See when, where and on what you catch fish, your best days and months, and how one year compares with the last. Set your own goals, chase a Big Year or a slam, and earn badges as you go.

Private by design

There's no account and no analytics. Your log, photos and fishing spots are kept on your phone and never sent to us. They leave it only when you choose: a backup or export file you save, or your phone's own Android backup. Speaking a catch uses your phone's speech service, which may process your voice online. Spots never appear on share cards. Read the privacy policy.

Questions

How do I join the test?

Email us the address of the Google account you use for Google Play on your Android phone. We'll add you to the closed test and reply with the link to install Sailfin. We use the address only for the test, and remove it from the tester list when you ask or the test ends.

What does it cost?

Nothing while it's in testing. Sailfin has no ads and no subscription.

Where does it work?

The field guide covers the U.S. and Canada: 529 species, with a range for each of the 64 states and provinces. You can log a fish anywhere.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Logging and the field guide work with no signal. Weather, tides and river flow fill in once you're back online, and the map needs a connection.

Does it show fishing regulations?

No. Tips in Sailfin are not regulations. Always check your state or province's current rules before you keep a fish.

What leaves my phone?

Your log, photos and spots are never sent to us. To look up conditions, Sailfin sends a rounded position for weather and river flow, a nearby station ID for tides, and map tile requests for the area you view. Speaking a catch uses your phone's speech service, and Android's own backup can include your log. The privacy policy has the details.

How do I move my log to a new phone?

In Sailfin, go to Settings → Your data → Back up and save the file somewhere you can reach. On the new phone, tap "New phone? Restore a backup" on the first screen, or Settings → Your data → Restore. Restoring replaces the log already on that phone, so if it has catches you want to keep, back it up first.

Where do the pictures and tips come from?

Paintings by U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service artists and old fisheries plates (public domain), and AI-made illustrations checked against each fish's field marks. Tips come from fish and wildlife agencies. Every source is on the credits page.
